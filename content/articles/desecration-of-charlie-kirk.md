Tyler Robinson didn't kill Charlie Kirk. He just pulled the trigger.

The truth is, Charlie was already marked for death by a class of people. The same people who marked Donald Trump for death. The same people who will mark you and those you love for death, if you become too much of a problem for them.

They don't really want to kill you. In fact, they'd rather not.

It's a messy business, this whole murder thing.

It's just, you really need to submit.

And by submission, I don't mean agreement. They really couldn't care less if you're a total goose-stepping fascist posting to 300 followers on twitter or a classical liberal sipping wine while you read Sowell by your fireplace. You're allowed to have whatever opinions you want.

What you're not allowed to have is power. Charlie got a little too close, and so he had to go.

It's not personal. It's politics.

## Act 1

I still can't decide if that quote is cope or not. I posted it because I wanted it to be true. If Charlie's gone, then his death must mean something. We have to make it mean something!

And I know that millions of you feel the same, because that post went viral in a way I've never experienced before, and am unlikely to ever experience again. I felt the raw power of Charlie's influence moving through my page the day he died.

I had been a fan of Charlie for years, passively consuming his content for entertainment. I liked him, but I was also critical of him. I was still very much in my losertarian phase. So focused on my principles that I couldn't see reality, and had zero sense of political strategy.

I actually thought I knew better than Charlie. I really believed that.

Then, they killed him, and it's like a switch flipped in my brain. The world started to make sense. The reality of politics became clear. And for the first time, I could actually see what Charlie was building.

So, who was this man they were so desperate to defeat?

The most dangerous man in American politics ran on Starbucks mint tea. Mint Majesty, two honeys, all day long… because he talked so much, it was the only thing that kept him from losing his voice.

He stood at 6'5" tall, and he liked to joke about how he had descended from the Nephilim, the giants of the Old Testament.

It started with a folding card table lodged in the basement of his parents' house. He lugged that thing to the nearest university with a homemade sign that said BIG GOVERNMENT SUCKS.

He was 18 years old, the age men can be drafted into war.

That little card table was the entire operation. That was Turning Point USA in 2012.

A decade later, that card table had become the largest conservative youth machine in America. Seven hundred employees. Seventy million dollars a year. Hundreds of chapters.

He filled arenas with the same kids the experts swore were gone for good. He turned out the youth vote that helped put Trump back in the White House… the President credited him for it himself.

Every year, those campuses produce the next generation of the progressive militia. The teachers, the journalists, the bureaucrats, the politicians, the judges… whoever holds this territory writes the future, and it was theirs, unchallenged for sixty years.

But then there was this damn kid, and his damn card table, setting up shop right in the middle of their territory…

And he just would not go away.

Everybody knows Charlie is a hateful person.

Thousands of them, turning in the sky at once.

That's the consensus. That's why Stephen King believed Charlie wanted to stone gays, and it's why Tyler Robinson killed Charlie Kirk. Nobody gave either of them an order. The flock doesn't give orders… it murmurs. And every bird adjusts.

They're just flying with their flock.

You probably know what this is like. If you are human, you are a social creature. You flock too. It's okay. We all do.

Tyler and Stephen weren't aware of this. They convinced themselves they were in control. That they had a firm grasp of the facts. They never saw the flock feeding them those facts… and those 'facts' turned one of them into a liar in front of millions, and the other into a corpse-maker in front of the world.

So who answers for it?

Robinson goes to prison. Fine. But the thing that made him, the thing that put the lie in Stephen's mouth and the target on Charlie's back… that thing gets to walk away clean and pick its next target. It always does.

The flock flies on.

Why does it get to keep flying? Why can't anyone stop it?

Because there's nothing to stop. The flock isn't physical, it's spiritual.

You can shoot a bird out of the sky. You can't shoot a belief. Kill the man who holds it, and it just moves to the man beside him. That's why the flock never breaks. You took Robinson out of it and it didn't even flinch.

The flock took out our most effective general, and your reward is locking up one confused young man who happened to pull the trigger. And even that, for you, is a struggle. You are tormented for months. Hoping you can get some small hint of justice… eventually.

This isn't the system failing you. It's the system telling you the truth. It's letting you know your place.

The one who can kill and answer to no one, that's the sovereign. That's who rules you. The one who has to beg the courts for months to sentence a single pawn… that's the subject. That's who is ruled.

The flock is your real Government, and it doesn't have a President or a Congress.

It has a vibe.

And sometimes, when that vibe feels threatened… it turns murderous.

Charlie threatened the vibe, so the vibe killed him.
