A reader posted in the comments that conservatives are "just as captured by their tribe" as progressives. The standard move would be to argue against it. I didn't. I conceded the surface and reframed underneath.

Watch what happens in the screenshot. Three moves in one sentence:

1. **Concede the visible.** "You're right, both sides have tribes." This is the four-dodge in action: refuse the trap of "defend my side." The argument they expected, I'm not making.

2. **Replace the metric.** "The question isn't who has tribes. It's whose tribe is built to predate and whose is built to be preyed on." The frame shifts from a symmetry game ("both sides bad") to an asymmetry question ("which side is the apex"). Their measuring tape gets swapped for mine.

3. **Hand them the conclusion.** "Pick one. Then ask which one you're in." I don't argue the answer. I let the question do the work. By the time they've considered it, they've already done the categorization themselves.

The reader didn't reply. Three other readers liked the comment. One of them DMed me asking how I learned to write that way.

You learn it by stopping the dodge. The first move, "you're right," is the hardest, because every instinct says don't yield. But yielding the surface is what lets you reframe the floor underneath.

That's the whole pattern: concede, reframe, return.
