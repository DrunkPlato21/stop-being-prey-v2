  Round one happened on a Facebook post about Charlie Kirk's murder.

  on top of each other, none of them engaging the original
  dismantling. Round two.
move: |
  The Time-Waste Trap. When the first attack fails, the bad-faith
  opponent comes back with volume. A second comment, twice as long,
  full of new claims that demand new dissection. The trap isn't the
  argument. The trap is the time-cost. Every minute Clay spends
  responding is a minute he's not writing, building, or talking to
  readers who matter. The wall of text is a tax.
trap: |
  The instinct is to dissect the new wall the same way you
  dissected the first. Treat round two like a continuation of round
  one. That's the trap. You become his content engine, working for
  free, while he extracts your attention as the payment. Each new
  "fact" he piles on is an invitation to spend an hour you don't
  have.
frame_shift: |
  Don't reply. When bad-faith opponents pile new walls of text on
  top of an already-won argument, they're trying to extract your
  time as payment. Stop paying.

  Walk away publicly. Let the audience see you walk. The move is
  not silence. It's visible refusal. Pin the moment as a
  demonstration and step out. The audience watches who blinks
  first, and they read the refusal as confidence, not retreat.

  For Clay specifically, this is the wall mechanic. The exchange
  that was already won becomes the engine of something the opponent
  can't take away. Readers fund the work, hostility converts to
  infrastructure. For most members, the move is simpler. Save the
  exchange for later. Use it as a story, a lesson, a Case File
  submission to this room. The hostility funds your understanding,
  not your wallet. That's still worth more than the time he was
  trying to drain.
one_shot: "Don't reply. Open a wall."
