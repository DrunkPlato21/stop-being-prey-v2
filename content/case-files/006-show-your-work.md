move: |
  Round one: The Credentialed Dodge. Two beats stacked into a
  single posture. Declare the answer obvious. Then refuse to
  give it. The trick is credentialism-by-tone. "Any intelligent
  folk" implies a club, and Ben is in it. The withholding does
  the work the argument cannot. He never engaged Sowell. He
  signaled membership in the room that knows better and walked
  off expecting the room to nod.

  Round two: The Frame Reset. The dodge failed in public. Ben
  had two options. Produce the work, on Clay's frame. Or switch
  frames. He switched. His second comment did two things, both
  classic. First, tone-policing: does your attitude make readers
  take you seriously? Second, burden-flipping: if you had to
  answer Sowell, what would you say? Try to be a good
  representative of your "side." Neither move addressed the
  demand still sitting on the table. Both were attempts to make
  the conversation about anything other than the missing
  answer.
trap: |
  Two traps, one per round.

  Round one: defend Sowell on substance. Work out which answer
  Ben might have meant and pre-empt it. Once you are arguing
  equal rights versus group-specific rights, you have conceded
  that Ben holds the obvious answer. You are guessing at his
  case for him, working both sides while he sits back as judge.

  Round two: accept the frame reset. Defend your tone. Or worse,
  actually answer Sowell on Ben's behalf to prove you can "be a
  good representative of your side." The instant you do either,
  you have moved off the original demand and the dodge has
  worked. The new frame becomes the conversation. The withheld
  answer is forgotten.
frame_shift: |
  Round one: take him at his word. He said the answer was
  obvious. Demand it. Refuse to argue the substance Sowell
  raised until Ben delivers the obvious answer he claimed to
  have.

  Then use his stated authority against him. Ben's profile says
  he is a former teacher. Every teacher in history has told
  students to show their work. Make him sit in the student's
  chair. He came in expecting to be the schoolteacher. Hand him
  the chalk and ask for the proof.

  Round two: refuse the reset. Don't acknowledge the new frame
  exists as a frame. Name what he just did out loud. Fifty more
  words, zero work. He switched to tone-policing because the
  first move failed. Then return to the original demand without
  blinking. The audience sees the goalpost move. They also see
  it refused. The demand stands. The work is still missing.

  The doctrine demonstrated across both rounds is the same. The
  opponent decides whether to do the work. The reader decides
  whether the opponent did it. Clay's job is to make sure the
  question never moves.
