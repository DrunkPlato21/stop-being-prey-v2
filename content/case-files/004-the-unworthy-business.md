situation: |
  I posted on the Thomas Sowell Quotes Facebook page about Sam
  Walton's lifelong opposition to the minimum wage. The thesis:
  the minimum wage is not a floor under wages, it is a moat
  around the biggest corporations. Walmart and Amazon can absorb
  wage hikes. The corner store cannot.

  Ryan Christopher arrived in the comments with a moral verdict.
move: |
  The Unworthy Business. Two beats stacked into a single
  judgment. Setup: your business depends on exploiting workers.
  Verdict: therefore your business is not important and does not
  deserve to exist.

  Ryan dressed the move as worker advocacy. It is not. It is a
  claim of authority over which businesses deserve to keep
  operating. The progressive issuing the verdict is the unstated
  central planner.
trap: |
  The instinct is to defend the business or litigate the studies.
  That's the trap. Defending concedes that Ryan gets to decide
  which businesses deserve to exist. Litigating the studies
  fights inside Ryan's frame and turns the thread into two guys
  arguing over numbers. The audience drifts.
frame_shift: |
  Refuse the moral frame. Reassign the word exploitation.
  Exploitation requires being trapped. Two adults agreeing on a
  wage is negotiation. The only party using coercion is the
  state, which outlaws the deal below a price floor.

  Walk the audience to the kid who cannot get hired. A
  sixteen-year-old whose labor is worth eight dollars an hour
  loses the job before he is ever offered one. The law made him
  too expensive to take a chance on. Ryan thinks he is helping
  this kid. He is the kid's problem.

  Then surface the lobbying. Walmart, Amazon, and Costco all
  publicly support minimum wage increases. The biggest
  corporations in the world want the moat Ryan is digging. The
  reply is not written for Ryan. It is written for the readers
  watching the thread.
one_shot: "Walmart, Amazon, and Costco publicly lobby for minimum wage increases. They disagree with me and agree with you. How does that sit?"
rules_applied: [2, 3, 6]

wall_screenshot_transcript: |
  CLAY'S REPLY:

  Ryan Christopher Hi Ryan. I'm glad you're here.

  Let's take a look at what you just said.

  You've described a voluntary exchange between two consenting adults as exploitation. **Can you point me to the actual exploitation?**

  Exploitation requires someone be trapped. The worker can walk away. The employer can choose not to hire. They're negotiating. The only party using coercion is the state, which makes the deal illegal at any price below the floor. **A minimum wage doesn't raise wages. It outlaws jobs below a certain price.**

  Think about that for a second. The minimum wage outlaws jobs, quite literally. Do you acknowledge that you are outlawing jobs when you support the minimum wage?

  We need to establish where you stand here.

  Picture the kid this law shuts out. A 16-year-old who wants his first job. He has no experience, he has no skills, but he wants an opportunity. His labor is maybe worth $5 an hour at best. Not because his boss is evil. Because he hasn't learned how to be valuable yet. An employer is willing to hire him at $8 and train him into someone worth $15.

  The minimum wage makes that deal illegal. Do you think that's right? If both of them consent, do you feel a duty to interfere? On whose behalf?

  **The kid didn't get exploited. He got locked out.** And the worst thing is, you think you're doing him a favor.

  Here's a question I really want you to sit with.

  **Walmart, Amazon, and Costco all publicly lobby for minimum wage increases.** Did you know that? They disagree with me and agree with you. How does that sit with you?

  One more question: did you know that the vast majority of jobs pay more than the minimum wage? Why would they do that, if they only legally have to pay the minimum? Is someone forcing them to pay more, or is there something else you need to consider?
